## Result and reopening condition

The bank emits 5028 bytes with seven differing non-relocation bytes at `+0x136C` through `+0x1372`. Retail contains `03 D1 8B 08 8B 75 F4`; the candidate contains `8B 75 F4 03 D1 8B 08`. Retail finishes the second variance sum with `add edx, ecx`, reads the current metric with `mov ecx, [eax]`, then reloads the loop index with `mov esi, [ebp-0x0C]`. The candidate reloads the index before those other two instructions. The instructions following `+0x1373` agree. Raw evidence is build/rva009b6d80-close/probe-bank.log, bank-measurement.json, bank.obj, bank-compiled.bin and bank-relocs.json.

The initial new hypothesis was that expressing the second metric through a scoped reference or an explicit current-value temporary would defer the independent loop-index reload. Neither spelling appears in the earlier five verdicts or their evidence notes. The refutation condition was an unchanged residue or additional differences. Both emit the same seven differing bytes, reaching the assignment's limit of two unchanged scheduling experiments. A guarded do-while also leaves that result unchanged. A visible variance helper and a barrier after the completed metric read produce worse bodies. The best bank is therefore preserved without submitting an equal or worse alternative. This refutes these tested levers, not every possible C++ reconstruction.

Reopening requires a distinct source or compiler mechanism that delays the index reload while preserving the scalar prefix and tail, or independent native source. Another scoped metric reference, explicit metric temporary, guarded do-while, this force-inlined sum helper or this post-metric-read barrier is not a new experiment. The remaining blocker is `codegen-order/scalar-loop`.
